This document reviews whether the current story can become a lovable children-first piano learning product, and converts the story into art-direction prompts for concept sketches.

## Short Verdict

The story has a good core, but the current version is still a skeleton. It can become appealing if every cute thing has a learning job.

Strong parts:

- dinosaurs are immediately understandable for young children;
- two homes becoming one gives a clear long-term emotional arc;
- high planet and low planet can map naturally to high/low sound later;
- the staff bridge is a distinctive mechanic if it feels like a real place.

Weak parts to fix:

- "build a base" can become generic if every level is just another object appearing;
- "high note dinosaur / low note dinosaur" is too adult-facing if introduced too early;
- the current story needs more desire, surprise, and tiny emotional turns;
- each note needs a stable reason to matter, not only a color or a glowing key.

Product judgement:

This can be popular with parents and young children only if it feels like a real children's game first and a music drill second. The wedge is not "more songs." The wedge is "my child finally understands what Do/C means, where it lives on the keyboard, where it sits on the staff, and why it makes the little dinosaur move."

## Market Fit Notes

Existing piano apps are strong in these areas:

- song libraries;
- microphone/MIDI feedback;
- adult and older-child progress paths;
- conventional sight-reading drills;
- score, stars, and lesson videos.

This project should not fight them head-on. Its better opening is:

- pre-reading and early-reading children;
- very small note sets;
- story consequences instead of worksheet questions;
- staff-reading as physical travel;
- original cute IP that can carry many short practice sessions.

Positioning sentence:

> A first piano literacy game for young children: notes become places, keys become homes, and the staff becomes a bridge.

## Story Quality Audit

### Does The Story Have A Child-Friendly Want?

Current answer: partly.

Better version:

星芽 is not just "repairing a base." The original young `星芽龙` has arrived to establish a new home in space and first needs a tiny safe moon outpost that can hold air and sing. Each note wakes one part of that shelter.

Later, 星芽 hears a low "dong" from far away. That sound is not a random next level. It is 咚咚 trying to build a home too. The long-term goal becomes friendship and connection.

Why this helps:

- children understand home, light, bridge, friend;
- the story gives a reason to continue;
- the high/low music concept arrives through emotion, not lecture.

### Is It Too Rough?

Yes, if left as:

- one level equals one object;
- press key, object appears;
- next level repeats the pattern.

Not rough if each level has a tiny dramatic beat:

- the floor is floating and 星芽 cannot stand;
- three lights are asleep and wake one by one;
- the rover wheel rolls away and must snap back;
- the bridge boards pop into place in the same direction as the melody;
- the wall is too high until Sol/G teaches the first big jump;
- the staff bridge is not a test strip, it is the road to another planet;
- the low planet answers with a warm "dong" that introduces 咚咚.

The difference is small but important: the child should see a problem before the note, then see the note solve that problem.

## Refined Core Metaphor

Use this mapping consistently:

| Music concept | Child story version | Visual behavior |
| --- | --- | --- |
| note name | the note's name tag | `Do/C`, `Re/D`, etc. appears briefly |
| keyboard locator | where the note lives | key group clue: two-black left, two-black middle |
| staff position | where the dino lands | note pad on a bridge line or space |
| sound | the note's voice | seed sings, piano tone replies |
| correct input | energy reaches the object | object moves, lights, grows, or opens |
| wrong input | wrong energy arrived | object wiggles gently, dino points to comparison |

## Character Refinement

### Xingya / 星芽

Core idea:

Original small bright `星芽龙`, informed by an agile bipedal ornithopod; first companion and curious builder of the new moon outpost.

Child-facing traits:

- likes to hop;
- collects star screws and tiny note seeds;
- gets excited when a sound makes something move;
- asks one-sentence hints.

Music role:

- first maps to the middle/high area;
- later becomes the right-hand/high-register buddy;
- should never be explained as "right hand only" on early child screens.

Visual silhouette:

- small round body with agile upright balance;
- expressive upturned balancing tail;
- exactly three soft teal sprout-like head buds, not a crown or hero crest;
- complete soft pressure suit, gloves, boots and airtight tail sleeve in vacuum; helmet-free explorer mode only after an atmosphere check;
- big readable eyes;
- tiny tool pouch shaped like a moon pocket.

Signature animation:

- ears/spikes perk up when hearing the target note;
- small hop on correct notes;
- soft stumble and quick recovery on wrong notes;
- bigger arc jump on staff bridge.

### Dongdong / 咚咚

Core idea:

Round triceratops, warm low-sound builder, steady friend.

Child-facing traits:

- strong but gentle;
- loves carrying big bridge stones;
- footstep makes soft low piano "dong";
- helps hold the bridge from below.

Music role:

- later maps to lower sounds and left-hand region;
- becomes bass/left-hand buddy;
- should be framed as capable and foundational, never clumsy or slow.

Visual silhouette:

- chubby triangle body;
- three soft rounded horns;
- large feet;
- warm cheek color;
- small star lantern on the horn or backpack.

Signature animation:

- happy foot stomp that makes a low note ripple;
- pushes a bridge support into place;
- nods when the child finds a low note;
- celebrates by making the ground glow.

## Note Identity Through Story

The story should assign recurring story jobs to notes, but avoid making children solve purely by object shape.

| Note | Identity link | Early story job | Later staff job | Common confusion repair |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Do/C | two-black left, starting note | first floor / home start | little ledger-line launch pad | compare with Re/D in two-black group |
| Re/D | two-black middle | wheel / middle light | space below staff | show it between C and E |
| Mi/E | two-black right | lamp / first bright star | first line | compare with Fa/F across black-key groups |
| Fa/F | three-black left | first new landing pad | first space | show it before three-black group |
| Sol/G | between first and second of three-black group | star gate / big jump | second line | compare F/G and C/G leap |

Key rule:

Color can decorate, but each note must be recognized by name, keyboard place, staff place, and sound over time.

## Chapter Refinement

Canonical story numbering is `月亮小家 -> 星星桥 -> 会听的小种子 -> 咚咚的低音星球 -> 会唱歌的大家园`. `音符护照` is a recurring collection/rest view, not a chapter. Detailed future level ids and teaching gates live in `03_CONTENT_ROADMAP.md`.

### Chapter 1: 月亮小家

Emotional promise:

Help 星芽 build the first tiny home.

Learning promise:

Find C-D-E-F-G on the keyboard with names and locators.

Scene:

A soft airless moon surface with toy-like craters, a sleepy unsealed house frame, a visible empty spot where parts will land, and Xingya in the complete soft pressure suit. M08 sealing the roof creates the first safe place to open the helmet.

Level rhythm:

1. Something is missing.
2. 星芽 notices and points.
3. One note wakes one part.
4. The part moves into place.
5. The new part reveals the next problem.

### Chapter 2: 星星桥

Emotional promise:

星芽 sees another planet and wants to reach it.

Learning promise:

Read a short staff as a bridge of landing pads.

Scene:

Left moon outpost, right small glowing planet, a short five-line staff bridge in between. Only one to two measures. The current note pad is large and touchable, and Xingya remains in the complete sealed suit throughout the open-space crossing.

Mechanic:

- correct note: 星芽 jumps to the exact staff pad;
- wrong note: 星芽 wobbles, shows the pressed note, then points to the target staff position and key group;
- finish: right planet opens automatically.

### Chapter 3: 会听的小种子

Emotional promise:

星芽 discovers seeds that sing.

Learning promise:

Connect sound to note name and key location.

Scene:

A small breathable garden on the new planet. Before the first seed task, the backpack checks the air and an automatic 2-4 second transition opens the helmet and removes the outer pressure layer. Each seed then sings one piano note and the child finds the matching key.

Mechanic:

- LS01-LS03 visibly model Do, Re, and Mi before the child echoes them;
- LS04-LS05 hide the target only inside a just-practiced C/D or C/D/E set;
- LS06 grows a long C/G vine before LS07 untangles neighboring Mi/Fa flowers;
- LS08 turns two-note echoes into lit roots, then ends with an unscored low answer from underground;
- correct changes rotate pods, open leaves, grow vines, separate flowers, or light roots instead of repeating the same sprout;
- wrong: the neutral central source repeats the unlabeled target piano sound and 星芽 names the child's pressed note gently; no candidate seed animates before the next response.

Teaching guardrail: this chapter must never be marketed or reported as absolute-pitch training. A modeled reference, a visible/heard home note, or a just-practiced candidate set remains part of every reduced-cue task.

### Chapter 4: 咚咚的低音星球

Emotional promise:

星芽 hears a warm low sound and finds 咚咚.

Learning promise:

Introduce lower register and later bass staff through a character job.

Scene:

A rounder, warmer planet with gentle caves, sturdy bridge supports, low glowing crystals, and 咚咚's half-built home.

Mechanic:

- first hear and compare low/high versions of a familiar note;
- low sounds move heavy pieces while higher sounds light small stars;
- the child finds the separate `C3-G3` keyboard home before bass staff or left-hand language appears;
- Dongdong is heard in LP01, appears first as a silhouette in LP04, and is fully revealed in LP05 before asking for left-hand help;
- the left hand walks the already-known low route in LP07, so hand identity and bass-staff reading do not arrive in the same first-exposure state;
- the underground staff bridge turns `C3-G3` bass positions into Dongdong's physical path.

Teaching guardrail: weight, character, and palette create emotion but cannot be the answer. Reduced-cue play must still require sound, key geography, note/register identity, or bass-staff position. A correct `Do/C` in the wrong octave is a near miss, not low-register mastery.

### Chapter 5: 会唱歌的大家园

Emotional promise:

Two small homes become one shared musical home.

Learning promise:

Prepare for two hands and grand staff.

Scene:

High planet and low planet connected by a larger music bridge. Central C becomes the meeting place.

Mechanic:

- 星芽 jumps on middle/high pads;
- 咚咚 holds or steps on low pads;
- the characters call and respond in turns before any simultaneous action;
- treble and bass maps connect around middle C;
- each part rehearses separately before one paired landing and a two-measure original duet.

Teaching guardrail: the 4-6 core path uses sequential role/register relay and opens the garden before any simultaneous invitation. Paired play is a later optional encore with sequential fallback, not an ability branch. Note input can prove pitch and timing, but cannot by itself prove which physical hand the child used; microphone remains sequential only.

Route guardrail: the relay finale and together finale open the same garden. The app does not show `easy/hard`, does not interrupt the child with a next-level dialog, and automatically returns a tired or struggling child to the relay route.

### Chapter 4-5 Strict Audit Result

Resolved design risks:

- Dongdong is now heard before silhouette, and silhouette before full reveal; the new companion no longer appears without story cause.
- Low sound, low key geography, left-hand prompting, bass-staff reading, and two-hand timing enter in separate steps.
- `C3` and `C4` share a note name but keep separate key homes, staff positions, and mastery evidence.
- Middle `C4` is the grand-staff meeting landmark; `C3` is not mislabeled as middle C.
- The final piece is fixed at two bars, with one rhythm pattern and one note sequence across all planning sources.
- The 4-6 route completes by relay; simultaneous play is a no-penalty bonus route with the same ending.
- Finger numbers remain adult support rather than another child-facing symbol system during Chapters 4-5.

Child-appeal rationale:

- LP missions alternate listening, searching, ordering stones, following echoes, revealing footprints, moving one large object, walking a hand route, reading a map, and crossing a bridge; they are not ten reskins of one construction tap.
- Dongdong's staged reveal creates curiosity, while each correct note causes an immediate visible world change.
- TH missions make coordination mean two friends cooperating, and the route fallback protects pride instead of presenting a failed two-hand exam.

Still unproven:

- no document can prove that 4-6 children understand or enjoy the flow;
- a piano teacher must review hand posture, register range, notation rendering, and the two-bar arrangement before implementation expansion;
- at least 3-5 real 4-6 children must be observed on the Chapter 4 vertical slice before the remaining LP/TH course is treated as child-validated.

## Concrete Level Polish Examples

### M01: 第一块月亮地板

Problem:

Xingya exits the ship in the complete sealed suit, but the floor tile is floating above the airless moon surface.

Teaching:

`Do/C` is the first home sound. It lives to the left of the two black keys.

Correct:

The floor tile drops softly. 星芽 stands on it and smiles.

Wrong:

The tile wiggles but does not land. Bubble: `这是 Re/D。找 Do/C。`

### M03: 小车轮回家

Problem:

The rover wheel keeps rolling past 星芽.

Teaching:

`Re/D` sits in the middle of the two-black-key group.

Correct:

The wheel rolls into the rover with a click.

Wrong:

If C is pressed, the wheel rolls too far left. If E is pressed, too far right. This makes the key location visible.

### M06: 大跳墙

Problem:

A wall is too high for tiny hops.

Teaching:

`Sol/G` is the first big leap from Do/C.

Correct:

`C-G-C` makes three wall blocks rise: start, jump, home.

Wrong:

If F is pressed instead of G, the wall rises halfway and slides back, then the three-black locator appears.

### S01: 星星桥第一跳

Problem:

The next planet is visible across open space, but there is no road except a five-line bridge. Xingya is fully sealed, including the tail sleeve.

Teaching:

Staff positions are jump pads: C ledger line, D below staff, E first line, F first space, G second line.

Correct:

星芽 physically jumps to the staff note pad. The bridge glows under the landing foot.

Wrong:

星芽 lands beside the pad, dusts off, and the app says what was pressed. Then it gives a staff clue and a key clue.

## Art Direction

Target feel:

- premium preschool game;
- polished children's book illustration;
- toy-like materials;
- soft but not muddy;
- readable on iPad from a child's distance;
- cute without becoming babyish for 7-8 year olds.

Avoid:

- realistic dinosaurs;
- hard sci-fi;
- dark empty space;
- generic clipart;
- anime/franchise resemblance;
- too many tiny decorative details near the notes;
- text embedded inside art assets.

Recommended style:

- rounded 3D storybook illustration;
- soft clay / vinyl toy surface;
- gentle rim lighting;
- clean silhouettes;
- saturated accent colors on neutral moon/space backgrounds;
- high readability around keyboard and staff.

## Design Gate Before Production Art

Before using generated images as actual app assets:

- confirm silhouettes work at small sizes;
- confirm no copyright/IP resemblance;
- confirm staff positions remain musically readable;
- confirm keyboard space can fit real black/white key proportions;
- confirm characters do not cover notes;
- confirm art is not solving the level by color alone;
- confirm the same art style can support idle, correct, wrong, and celebration states.
