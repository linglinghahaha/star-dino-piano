# Product North Star

## One-line Positioning

`Star Dino Piano` is a story-first iPad piano primer for young children: a small dinosaur crosses space bridges by matching solfege, letter names, staff positions, keyboard locations, and real piano sounds.

## Product Promise

The product is not "another song library piano app." Its first job is to help a child build the earliest mental links:

- `Do / C`
- "two-black-key left"
- staff position
- heard piano sound
- one simple action in a story world

If a feature does not strengthen one of these links, it is secondary.

## Target User

- Child: roughly 4-8 years old, early or pre-reading piano learner.
- Parent: wants low-friction practice without needing to explain every screen.
- Teacher: may use it as a light supplement for note-name, key-finding, and staff-recognition drills.

Internal age bands:

- 4-6: pre-reading friendly, large targets, minimal text, parent setup, and no required English word reading.
- 6-8: can handle more letter-name practice, short labels, staff-position language, and later optional A-G word play.

Child-facing screens should be designed for the younger band first. Parent and teacher surfaces can carry denser explanations.

## Teaching Assumption

The MVP uses fixed-do mapping:

- `Do = C`
- `Re = D`
- `Mi = E`
- `Fa = F`
- `Sol = G`

Do not introduce movable-do, transposition, or alternate solfege systems until the first C-D-E-F-G course is stable.

## Core Differentiation

Most piano learning apps compete on song catalog, performance feedback, and adult/beginner lesson breadth. This project should compete on:

- early note identity before complex songs;
- staff as a playable world, not a worksheet;
- touch-first play, with MIDI and acoustic listening as optional growth paths;
- original IP-safe character world;
- tiny learning steps with visible story consequences.

## Must-not-drift Rules

- Do not make the main loop only "follow the glowing key."
- Do not make color the final answer. Color is an early scaffold.
- Do not use copyrighted characters, melodies, or brand imagery.
- Do not add large text explanations into the child-facing screen.
- Do not prioritize many songs before the child can connect note name, key, staff, and sound.

## MVP Success Definition

The MVP is successful when a child can:

1. Recognize `Do/C`, `Re/D`, `Mi/E`, `Fa/F`, `Sol/G` in a small set.
2. Find those notes on the on-screen or real piano keyboard.
3. Understand that a staff note has a physical place on the bridge.
4. Get clear correct/wrong feedback without adult explanation.
5. Finish a short story chapter with the dinosaur reaching a visible destination.

## Later Expansion

The A-G word typing game is a later project. It belongs after the core C-D-E-F-G note identity loop works reliably.

## Input Priority

MVP priority is:

1. Touch input: always available.
2. MIDI: optional on web, stronger in a future native iPad build.
3. Microphone listening: optional and limited to simple note sets until reliability is proven.

Do not market MIDI or microphone as universal in the web prototype.
