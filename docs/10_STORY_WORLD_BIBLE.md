# Story World Bible

## Core Frame

The child is helping an original young space dinosaur establish a new home, beginning with an unsealed moon outpost. Each piano note carries energy that moves a physical object in the world. After the first safe shelter is built, the dinosaur crosses a staff-shaped star bridge to search for a breathable garden, a friend, and the foundations of a shared home.

## Why Piano Notes Matter In The Story

Notes are not arbitrary buttons. In this world:

- a note name tells the dinosaur which energy to use;
- the key location is where that energy is found;
- the staff position is where the dinosaur can land;
- correct sound moves the world forward.

The story must always reinforce the music concept.

## Main Character

Default name: `星芽`.

English/internal alias: `Xingya`. Avoid using `Star Dino` as a live child-facing character name unless the branding plan is deliberately changed.

Canonical species: `星芽龙`, an original extraterrestrial young dinosaur whose body plan is informed by a small agile bipedal ornithopod. Xingya is not a Tyrannosaurus, sauropod, triceratops, or a claimed reconstruction of any named fossil species.

Environment rule: Xingya needs breathable air, temperature control, and pressure. Moon-surface and open-space scenes therefore use a complete lightweight pressure suit, including gloves, boots, life-support backpack, and an airtight tail sleeve. Helmet-free play begins only after a habitat is pressurized or the garden atmosphere is checked. See `27_XINGYA_SPECIES_AND_SPACE_GEAR_BIBLE.md`.

Role:

- friendly guide;
- reacts to the child's input;
- gives short contextual hints;
- physically moves when notes are correct.

Allowed moods:

- point;
- listen;
- happy;
- try again;
- celebrate;
- jump.

Do not imitate any existing TV, film, anime, superhero, or toy character.

## Character Voice And Record-Ready Copy

Xingya and Dongdong must remain recognizable even when the speaker label is hidden:

- Xingya is curious, quick, and discovery-led. A rare `咦`, `哇`, or `嘘` may open a moment, but it cannot become a repeated verbal tic. Before listening, Xingya raises all three head sprouts as a visual signature and uses a plain invitation such as `嘘，先听。`;
- Dongdong is slower, lower, and concise. Short pauses and grounded confirmations such as `接住了。` distinguish Dongdong from Xingya; Dongdong does not shout, grade the child, or turn weight and stability into pitch clues;
- The current breathable ecology planet is formally named `森芽星`; this names the existing garden-and-cave world and does not add a destination or route;
- Dongdong comes from `鸣石星` and currently guards Senya's underground `共鸣石前哨`. Season One does not travel to Mingshi, and the current cave is not Dongdong's birthplace;
- both characters describe the current world change instead of giving generic praise. Dialogue never replaces the visible action that a non-reading child needs;
- ordinary UI and keyboard surfaces use letter names. Solfege may be spoken only by a character;
- recording-ready lines are literal fixed sentences. They never contain runtime placeholders such as `<pressed letter>` or `<current solfege>`. Dynamic pressed-note identity stays in ordinary UI; the character follows with a fixed, target-specific repair line;
- optional voice ends before a teaching-piano onset and never delays an explicit replay.

## Chapter Structure

### Chapter 1: Moon Base Builder

Purpose: key finding and first note identities.

Story beats:

- find Do/C to wake up the floor;
- use C-D-E to turn on small lights;
- hear D/Re and wake the singing wheel;
- build short bridge pieces;
- move down E-D-C for rocket lights;
- make the first leap C-G-C;
- complete a memory light string;
- climb C-D-E-F-G to close the roof.

Level story contract:

| Level | Story need | Correct result | Teaching anchor |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| M01 | the base needs its first floor | floor lands under the dinosaur | `Do/C`, two-black left |
| M02 | the base needs three small lights | lights wake up in C-D-E order | two-black left/middle/right |
| M03 | the moon rover wheel sings, then echoes home | wheel wakes up and rolls in | hear `Re/D`, then `Do/C`, and find each key |
| M04 | a small bridge must connect | bridge grows left to right | melodic direction on nearby keys |
| M05 | rocket lights must count down | lights move E-D-C | descending direction |
| M06 | the base wall needs a big jump | wall rises through C-G-C | `Sol/G` locator and leap |
| M07 | star lights test memory | stars light without large cue | reduced scaffold |
| M08 | roof closes the base | roof climbs C-D-E-F-G | F/G preview before staff |

Every level should end with a visible state change that explains why the next level exists.

### Chapter 2: Staff Star Bridge

Purpose: staff recognition.

Story beats:

- the completed moon outpost is the start;
- five-line staff is the bridge;
- every note pad is a landing place;
- correct input makes the dinosaur jump;
- wrong input makes a gentle stumble and hint;
- the ecology-planet arrival is the visible finish.

The bridge is not a quiz strip. It is the path to the next planet:

- the moon outpost shows "where we came from";
- the ecology-planet destination shows "where we are going";
- each staff note is a physical landing pad;
- the dinosaur must visibly move from pad to pad;
- the final jump should open the ecology-planet arrival automatically.

This first bridge is a temporary exploration route for one sealed traveler. It proves the destination is reachable, but it is not yet the permanent shared-home structure built in Chapter 5 and cannot carry the later high/low cooperation by itself.

### Chapter 3: Listening Seed

Purpose: connect real piano sound to note identity.

Story beats:

- the first three seeds visibly model C, D, and E before asking for an echo;
- familiar seeds then hide their names and sing within a just-practiced two- or three-note set;
- hidden calls come from one neutral echo seed/core. Candidate flowers, near/far stones, Xingya gestures, stereo position, and plant motion remain equal until the child answers, so the garden never reveals pitch through where something moved;
- a distant C/G vine comes before the neighboring E/F flowers;
- the child echoes two sequential C-D-E sounds only after single-note comparison is familiar;
- correct sounds turn, open, untangle, grow vines, and light roots so the garden does not repeat one sprout animation;
- later MIDI or microphone input can feed the same loop without replacing touch.

This is guided small-set pitch matching, not an absolute-pitch test. After LS08 is complete, one root sings middle C and receives a much lower unscored answer. The ground responds instead of the sky. The child is not asked to find the low key yet; this is the story bridge into Chapter 4, so Dongdong does not appear as an unrelated new mascot.

### Cross-chapter System: Note Passport

The note passport is a collection and rest point, not a numbered chapter. A passport page unlocks after play and shows the note's sound, name, key home, staff home, and story memory. It never replaces active play and never claims mastery from one guided completion. Its proposed naming-carrier review is a separate teacher-gated opening action: Xingya calls a learned solfege, the child finds the matching neutral letter stamp, and one later guided key delivery returns to the current story. Full story, cue and evidence boundaries live in `docs/71` and remain runtime-forbidden.

### Chapter 4: Dongdong and the Underground Echo Cave

Purpose: experience high/low first, then learn low `C3-G3`, bass-staff positions, and left-hand readiness.

Story beats:

- a low echo opens the `地下回声洞` below the breathable garden on Senya, the current ecology planet;
- Xingya finds Dongdong, a warm, low-wide visitor from `鸣石星` who currently guards the cave's `共鸣石前哨`;
- the child first compares high and low sounds without hand or clef terminology;
- low notes find a separate keyboard home around `C3-G3`;
- only after the low key area is familiar does Dongdong invite the left hand to walk an already-known route;
- Dongdong's underground five-line map then turns that familiar route into the bass staff;
- the completed low bridge sends a signal to Xingya's home.

`咚咚的低音星球` remains the Chapter 4 musical nickname. It is not Mingshi's formal name, and Season One does not add a Mingshi map node, flight, or bridge endpoint. After the LP05 teaching action and world change are complete, Dongdong may say `我从鸣石星来。`; the line does not teach a note, trigger navigation, or advance mastery.

Story rule: "heavy/steady" is a sensory metaphor for register, not the answer. The child must still use sound, keyboard geography, note identity, and later staff position.

### Chapter 5: Two Homes Sing Together

Purpose: prepare hand alternation, simple two-hand coordination, and the grand staff through cooperation.

Story beats:

- Dongdong sends a low `Do`, Xingya answers with the middle `Do`;
- Xingya answers from the breathable garden relay; the distant moon outpost appears as a remote signal endpoint, so the scene does not put an unsealed character back into vacuum;
- the two characters take turns before they ever play together;
- treble and bass staves appear as two connected maps;
- each hand rehearses its own tiny job separately;
- one paired landing locks the first shared arch;
- a two-measure original duet makes the two homes one musical garden.

The Chapter 5 bridge is the permanent high/low habitat and music link. It is distinct from S01's short exploration route even when both use staff lines as the child-facing metaphor.

Story rule: every 4-6 child first opens the garden through the relay finale. Simultaneous paired play appears only on later visits as a bonus encore with equal sequential fallback; it is not a failure gate, ability tier, or superior story reward. Note input alone does not prove which physical hand the child used.

## Reward Loop

Rewards should be visible story changes, not only stars or scores:

- object lands;
- light turns on;
- bridge grows;
- dinosaur reaches a new pad;
- destination opens.

Stars can exist as progress indicators, but they should not replace the story consequence.

## Writing Rules

- Use one short sentence per child-facing hint.
- Avoid abstract phrases like "build musical ability."
- Avoid repeated instruction blocks.
- Prefer ordinary UI `C -> 2-black left`.
- Let the dinosaur bubble carry the solfege connection, for example `我唱 Do，你来弹 C`, instead of putting `Do/C` in the top UI.
- Keyboard labels, route pads, task cards, staff hints, feedback, effects, results, map labels, and ordinary objects use letter names only.

## Visual Rules

- The character, target, and destination must be obvious in the first viewport.
- The staff bridge should feel like a place.
- Objects should look touchable and toy-like.
- Do not overuse dark space backgrounds if music targets become hard to read.
- Environment state must read consistently: complete pressure suit in vacuum, opened helmet in a pressurized habitat, and no outer suit only after the breathable-garden check.
